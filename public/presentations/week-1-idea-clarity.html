Foundation

Most founders fail not because their idea is bad, but because they haven't clearly defined what problem they're solving.

Framework

Problem → Idea → Solution

Case Study · Uganda

Safe Transport at Night

Problem
Riders can't safely hail transport at night in Kampala.
Initial Idea
Build a ride-hailing app.
Real Solution
Radio dispatch system with trusted driver vetting.

Key insight: The problem wasn't "no app exists" — it was "people don't trust unknown drivers at night."

The solution addressed trust, not technology. The real problem was never about software.

Mindset

Three Mindset Pillars

⚡
Bias Toward Action Move forward without perfect information. Ship 80% instead of planning 100%. Perfect analysis is the enemy of progress.
🔄
Learning Loops Rapid cycles of Hypothesis → Test → Learn → Iterate. Each loop should take days, not months.
🌊
Embrace Uncertainty Accept that you don't and can't know everything. Ambiguity is your operating environment, not a bug.
Validation

False Signals vs Real Signals

False Signals
"I had this problem once."
"People on Twitter talk about it."
"Everyone I ask says it's a good idea."
"My friends would use this."
Real Signals
People spend money trying to solve it.
They ask "How much?" not "That's cool."
The problem is urgent or frequent.
Customers have already built workarounds.
Deliverable

Your Problem Statement

For [target customer], the problem of [problem description] results in [negative outcome]. Currently, they [current workaround], which costs them [time / money / effort].

Be specific. Avoid vague language like "easier" or "better." Focus on the concrete outcome your customer wants.
