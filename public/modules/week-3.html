Foundation

The Channel Problem

Most founders build a product then wonder where to find customers. The best founders figure out the channel FIRST, then build the product to fit it.

Channel First

Your distribution channel determines your product shape. A WhatsApp-based service looks different from a marketplace.

Unit Economics

If it costs more to acquire a customer than they'll ever pay you, you don't have a business. You have an expensive hobby.

🔄

The Flywheel

Great GTM strategies create loops: each customer brings the next. Word of mouth isn't luck — it's designed.

Uganda Case Studies

📱

WhatsApp Commerce

How a Kampala food delivery service grew to 500 orders/week using WhatsApp groups instead of building an app. Cost per acquisition: UGX 0.

The Channel

WhatsApp groups organised by neighbourhood. No app download required. No marketing spend. Customers shared the group link themselves.

Why It Worked

WhatsApp was already on every phone. The ordering process was a simple message. Group dynamics created social proof — when one person ordered, others followed.

Key Insight

The best channel is the one your customers already use every day. Don't make them download something new.

🤝

Agent Networks

A solar company discovered that village agents outsold their website 40:1. The channel was the product.

The Channel

Local agents — trusted community members who earned commission on every sale. They demonstrated the product in person, handled objections, and collected mobile money payments.

Why It Worked

Rural customers don't buy from websites. They buy from people they know. The agent network turned a hardware product into a relationship-based sale.

Key Insight

In low-trust, low-internet environments, human distribution beats digital distribution every time.

⛪

Church & Community Groups

A savings platform grew by partnering with church savings circles (munno mukabi). Trust was the distribution channel.

The Channel

Existing church-based savings groups — munno mukabi — with established trust, regular meetings, and built-in accountability. The platform digitised what they were already doing.

Why It Worked

The groups already met weekly. Money conversations were already happening. The platform didn't need to create trust — it borrowed it from the church community.

Key Insight

Don't build communities from scratch. Find where your customers already gather and serve them there.

Framework

Building Your Go-to-Market Plan

A GTM plan answers three questions: Who are you selling to? How will you reach them? What will it cost?

The GTM Framework · Select a Tab
📡

Channels That Work in Uganda

Rank channels by cost and reach: WhatsApp, Facebook, agent networks, market days, radio, community groups, referral programs. Not every channel works for every product.

Low Cost, High Trust

WhatsApp groups, church/community networks, referral programs, word of mouth. These cost almost nothing but require you to be embedded in the community.

Medium Cost, Wide Reach

Facebook ads, market day activations, agent networks, radio spots. These scale but require cash. Test with small budgets first.

The Rule

Start with the channel closest to your customer. If your persona is a market vendor in Owino, don't start with Instagram ads. Go to Owino.

💵

Pricing & Revenue Model

Freemium, subscription, pay-per-use, one-time purchase. Mobile money pricing considerations. Round to clean numbers. Test willingness to pay.

Common Models

Freemium (free tier + paid upgrade), subscription (weekly/monthly via mobile money), pay-per-use (per transaction or per service), one-time purchase (hardware, training).

Uganda-Specific Considerations

Mobile money transaction fees eat into small payments. Round prices to clean numbers (5,000 UGX, not 4,750). Weekly payments often work better than monthly for low-income customers. Test the price before you build the product.

The Test

"Would you pay 10,000 UGX for this?" is a bad question. "Here is my mobile money number — send 10,000 UGX and I'll deliver it Friday" is the real test.

🏃

The 90-Day Sprint

Three months. Three milestones. From first customer to system.

Month 1: Validate

Pick one channel. Reach 50 people. Get 10 paying customers. Learn what works and what doesn't. Your only goal: prove someone will pay you.

Month 2: Optimise

Calculate your real CAC and LTV. Double down on what works, cut what doesn't. Get to 50 paying customers. Start tracking unit economics weekly.

Month 3: Systematise

Build repeatable processes. Can someone else run this channel without you? Document your sales process. Prepare to scale or add a second channel.

The GTM Funnel

Every customer passes through these four stages. Your GTM plan must address each one. Most founders only think about awareness — but conversion and retention are where the money is.

Framework

The Numbers That Matter

Unit economics separate real businesses from expensive hobbies. Know these numbers before you scale.

Dangerous Assumptions

❌
"We'll figure out monetisation later"
❌
"If we get enough users, revenue will follow"
❌
"Our customer acquisition cost is zero because we use social media"
❌
"We'll undercut competitors on price to win market share"
❌
"Everyone we talked to said they'd pay"

How to Think About Unit Economics

✅
Know your CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) before you scale
✅
Know your LTV (Lifetime Value) — how much does one customer generate?
✅
LTV must be at least 3x CAC for a sustainable business
✅
Price based on value delivered, not cost of production
✅
"Would you pay?" is not the same as "Here is my payment link"
