Foundation

The Brand Gap

Your brand isn't your logo. It's the gut feeling people have when they hear your name. Most early-stage founders skip branding entirely or waste money on a logo before they know who they're talking to.

🪞

Brand = Perception

What people say about you when you're not in the room. You don't control your brand — you influence it.

💬

Story Before Design

Your brand story comes from your customer's pain. The best brands narrate the transformation from problem to solution.

🏷️

Price Is Brand

How you price signals who you're for. Cheap isn't a strategy — it's a race to the bottom. Value-based pricing starts here.

SafeBoda's Trust Play

How SafeBoda differentiated from regular boda-bodas not through technology but through safety branding — helmets, insurance, and a name that led with the benefit.

The Brand Move

SafeBoda didn't build a better motorcycle. They built a better promise. The name itself — SafeBoda — tells the customer exactly what they get before they even download the app.

Why It Worked

Helmets, reflective jackets, and insurance weren't just operational decisions — they were brand decisions. Every rider became a walking billboard for trust.

Key Insight

Your brand is what you do, not what you say. SafeBoda's brand was visible on every street corner — no billboard needed.

🌯

Rolex Street Food

Uganda's beloved rolex (rolled eggs) has no brand owner, yet it's the most recognised street food in East Africa. What unbranded products teach about product-market fit.

The Paradox

No company owns the rolex. No trademark. No packaging. Yet every Ugandan knows what a rolex is, where to get one, and how much it costs. That's product-market fit so strong it doesn't need branding.

What It Teaches

Before you invest in logos and colour palettes, ask: is my product so good that people talk about it without being asked? The rolex spreads by word of mouth because the experience is consistent and delicious.

Key Insight

The best brand is a product people can't stop talking about. Get the product right first — then amplify with branding.

📶

MTN vs Airtel

How two telecom giants compete on brand positioning in Uganda. MTN owns "everywhere you go." Airtel fights on price. Who wins?

MTN's Position

MTN positioned on ubiquity and reliability. Yellow is everywhere — on shops, umbrellas, walls. "Everywhere you go" isn't just a slogan; it's a promise of network coverage. They own the premium position.

Airtel's Position

Airtel fights on price — cheaper data bundles, promotional offers, aggressive discounts. They position as the value alternative. But price wars erode margins and train customers to wait for the next deal.

Key Insight

The brand that owns a clear position in the customer's mind wins long-term. Competing on price alone is a race to the bottom. What position will you own?

Framework

Building Your Brand Position

A brand position is the space you occupy in your customer's mind. It answers: For whom? What category? What benefit? Why believe us?

Three Frameworks · Select a Tab
🎯

The Positioning Statement

A simple template that forces clarity. Every word earns its place.

The Template

For [target customer] who [need/problem], [brand name] is the [category] that [key benefit] because [reason to believe].

How to Use It

Walk through each element one at a time. Target customer comes from your Week 2 persona. Need/problem comes from your Week 1 problem statement. Category defines your competitive set. Key benefit is one thing — not five. Reason to believe is your proof.

Common Trap

Trying to be everything to everyone. If your positioning statement works for any company in your space, it's too generic. Be specific enough that your competitors can't say the same thing.

🗣️

Brand Voice & Personality

Are you the Expert? The Friend? The Rebel? The Guide? Pick one archetype. Everything flows from it.

Pick One Archetype

The Expert — authoritative, data-driven, precise. The Friend — warm, approachable, conversational. The Rebel — bold, provocative, challengers. The Guide — wise, supportive, empowering. Your archetype shapes how you write, speak, design, and price.

The Voice Test

Write three sentences in your chosen voice. Read them aloud. If they could have been written by any company, try again. Your voice should be recognisable even without your logo attached.

Why It Matters

Customers buy from brands that feel consistent. If your website sounds corporate but your WhatsApp sounds casual, you're creating confusion. Pick a lane and stay in it.

🎨

Visual Identity Basics

You don't need a branding agency yet. You need just enough to look consistent and intentional.

What You Need Now

A name. A colour palette (2-3 colours). A font pairing (one for headings, one for body). A consistent tone. That's enough to start. Everything else — logo, brand book, style guide — can come later.

Quick Wins

Use Canva to create a simple wordmark. Pick colours from Coolors.co. Choose Google Fonts that are free and load fast. Consistency beats perfection — use the same colours and fonts everywhere.

Common Trap

Spending UGX 2M on a logo before you've made UGX 1 in revenue. Your visual identity should cost you time, not money, at this stage. The logo can evolve — your positioning shouldn't.

The Brand Diamond

Your brand diamond connects four elements. Purpose drives everything. Promise faces the customer. Personality shapes how you communicate. Position defines where you stand relative to alternatives.

Framework

Pricing That Works

Most founders underprice because they're afraid of rejection. Here's how to set prices that reflect your value — and that your customers will actually pay.

Pricing Mistakes Founders Make

❌
Pricing based on what it costs you to make
❌
Copying competitor pricing without understanding their cost structure
❌
Offering the lowest price to "win" customers
❌
One price for everyone, regardless of value received
❌
Changing price every week based on who's complaining

How to Price With Confidence

✅
Price based on the value you create for the customer
✅
Test willingness to pay during customer interviews — don't guess
✅
Create 2-3 tiers: anchor high, sell the middle
✅
Round to mobile money-friendly numbers (avoid odd figures)
✅
Your price communicates your quality. Own it.

Action

Your Homework This Week

Define Your Brand & Test Your Pricing

This week you move from strategy to identity. Build your brand guide, set your pricing tiers, and test them with real customers before Week 5.

01

Write Your Positioning Statement

Use the template. Read it aloud. If it sounds like corporate jargon, simplify.

02

Choose Your Brand Archetype

Pick one. Write three sentences in that voice. Does it feel like you?

03

Design a Simple Visual Identity

Pick 2 colours, 1 font pair, and a simple wordmark. Use Canva if you need to.

04

Set Your Pricing Tiers

Basic, Standard, Premium. Make the middle tier the obvious choice.

05

Test Your Price

Go back to 5 interview contacts. Show them the offer and the price. Watch their reaction.

06

Map Your Revenue Model

Subscription? Per-transaction? Freemium? What makes sense for your customer's cash flow?

07

Write Your Origin Story

Two paragraphs: why you started this, and why you're the right person to solve it.
