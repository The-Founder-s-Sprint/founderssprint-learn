Foundation

The Pitch Problem

Most founders pitch their product. Investors fund stories. The difference between a deck that gets filed and one that gets funded is narrative structure — and the confidence to deliver it.

📖

Story Over Slides

Nobody remembers slide 7. Everyone remembers the founder who made them feel the problem. Lead with narrative, not features.

⏱️

3 Minutes, Not 30

If you can't explain your business in 3 minutes, you don't understand it well enough. Brevity is clarity.

🎤

Delivery Is Half the Pitch

The same deck delivered with conviction vs. hesitation produces completely different outcomes. Practice is not optional.

Fenix International (Now ENGIE)

How a solar energy company pitched its way from a Kampala apartment to $150M+ in funding.

The Story

Fenix didn't sell solar panels. They sold energy access for the unbanked. Their pitch reframed a hardware product as a financial inclusion story — and that's what opened cheque books.

Key Insight

The pitch that wins isn't about what you build — it's about the world you're creating for your customer.

🏍️

Tugende's Motorcycle Financing

Tugende pitched asset financing for boda-boda riders. The deck that won wasn't about loans — it was about dignity and ownership.

The Story

Tugende reframed motorcycle leasing as a pathway to ownership and economic independence. Instead of leading with interest rates and repayment terms, they led with a rider's journey from renting to owning. Story structure made the difference.

Key Insight

The same financial product, pitched as "loans" vs. pitched as "dignity," produces completely different investor responses.

🛵

SafeBoda's Pivot Pitch

When SafeBoda pivoted from safety to super-app, they had to re-pitch their entire vision.

The Story

SafeBoda started as a safe motorcycle taxi service. When they pivoted to a super-app model — adding payments, deliveries, and e-commerce — they had to reframe without losing the credibility they'd built. Their pitch threaded the needle: "We earned trust through safety. Now we're monetising that trust across services."

Key Insight

Pivots require re-pitching. The founders who do it well connect the old story to the new one — they don't erase it.

Framework

The 10-Slide Pitch Deck

Every great pitch deck follows a structure. You can be creative within it, but don't skip the essentials.

The Deck Structure · Select a Tab
🎬

Slides 1-4: The Setup

This is where you earn attention.

Slide 1: Cover

Your company name and a one-line hook. That's it. No paragraphs, no team photos, no logos of partners you don't have yet.

Slide 2: Problem

The pain you've validated. Use the problem statement you wrote in Week 1. Make the audience feel it.

Slide 3: Solution

Your approach — not every feature. One clear sentence about what you do and how it solves the problem.

Slide 4: Market

TAM/SAM/SOM from Week 2. Show that the opportunity is big enough to matter.

⚙️

Slides 5-7: The Engine

This is where you earn trust.

Slide 5: Business Model

How you make money. Be specific: "We charge X per Y" is better than "We monetise through multiple revenue streams."

Slide 6: Traction

What you've done so far — even if it's 10 customers, 50 interviews, or a working prototype. Honesty about where you are builds credibility.

Slide 7: Go-to-Market

Your 90-day plan from Week 3. How will you reach your first 100 customers? Show a concrete path, not a dream.

💰

Slides 8-10: The Ask

This is where you earn investment.

Slide 8: Team

Why you're the right people to solve this problem. Relevant experience, complementary skills, and — most importantly — proximity to the customer.

Slide 9: Financials

Unit economics, revenue projections, and key assumptions. Investors know your numbers will be wrong — they want to see that you understand the mechanics.

Slide 10: The Ask

What you need and what you'll do with it. "We're raising $X to achieve Y in Z months." Specific, reasonable, and tied to milestones.

The Pitch Arc

Every great pitch follows this emotional arc. You hook them, build tension around the problem, paint a compelling vision, prove you can deliver, then make a clear ask.

Framework

Delivering With Confidence

Pitch Day is not a presentation — it's a performance. Here's what separates founders who get meetings from founders who get polite applause.

Pitch Day Killers

❌
Reading from your slides word-for-word
❌
Starting with "So, um, my name is..."
❌
Spending 8 minutes on the problem and 30 seconds on the solution
❌
No practice — "I'll just wing it"
❌
Defensive responses to questions

What Judges and Investors Look For

✅
Confidence without arrogance — you know your business
✅
A clear hook in the first 15 seconds
✅
Evidence that you've talked to real customers
✅
Honest about what you don't know yet
✅
A specific, reasonable ask — not "we need funding"

Action

Your Homework This Week

Build Your Deck & Rehearse Your Pitch

This is it. Five weeks of work distilled into a 3-minute pitch and a 10-slide deck. The only way to be ready is to build it, practise it, and refine it.

01

Build Your 10-Slide Deck

Use Google Slides, Canva, or PowerPoint. One idea per slide. No walls of text.

02

Write Your Script

Not to read, but to internalise. Write out what you'll say for each slide.

03

Time Yourself

3 minutes for the pitch. 2 minutes for Q&A. Practice with a stopwatch.

04

Rehearse With Someone

Pitch to a friend, family member, or fellow founder. Get honest feedback.

05

Prepare for Q&A

List the 5 hardest questions you could be asked. Write your answers.

06

Refine Your Story Arc

Does your pitch have a clear hook, tension, vision, proof, and ask?

07

Rest Before Pitch Day

Seriously. Sleep well. Eat well. Show up as your best self.
